Reproducible studies on public data

Two studies. Each has a protocol fixed before any model was fitted, baselines before anything complex, fixed and fingerprinted splits, tests, and a log of every decision, including the ones that turned out wrong.

Both ask the same thing from different sides: what can a model be trusted to say when its data is incomplete, its labels are noisy, or the place it is used differs from the place it was trained?

River early warning across 671 British catchments

  • CAMELS-GB v2
  • Forecasting
  • Unseen regions
  • Uncertainty
  • In progress
{% comment %} TODO: REPLACE (Paul): the research question in one sentence, and what you expected to find, dated. {% endcomment %}

Setup

  • The question behind the design: how far ahead can river flow and flood or low-flow warnings be forecast without knowing future weather, and does that skill carry over to places the model was not trained on?
  • Daily river flow and weather for 671 catchments, 1970 to 2022. 659 had enough data to use.
  • Task: on day t, forecast flow 1, 3 and 7 days ahead, using only what is known on day t. No future rainfall.
  • Train on 1970 to 2004, choose settings on 2004 to 2012, test on 2012 to 2022.
  • Warnings: flow above a catchment's 95th percentile (high) or below its 10th (low), with thresholds taken from the training years.

  • Most of the skill beyond a day depends on knowing the weather. At 7 days the model reaches 0.24 without future weather and 0.72 with it. Median NSE at 7 days is 0.242 for per-catchment gradient boosting with no weather after the day the forecast is made, and 0.722 for the same model given the observed weather over the forecast window. The second figure is an upper bound, not a forecast.
  • A whole unseen region costs little. One regional model, tested on regions held out of training, loses a median of 0.019 NSE at 1 day. Five geographic folds, each region forecast by a regional model trained on the other four. Median NSE at 1 day falls from 0.762 to 0.723. The median loss per catchment is 0.019.
  • A different climate costs much more. Trained on wet catchments and tested on dry ones, NSE falls from 0.850 to 0.752. A random-half control loses about 0.03. A regional model trained on the wetter half of the catchments and tested on the drier half scores NSE 0.752, against 0.850 for the model trained on all of them. A random-half control loses about 0.03. Low-flow CSI falls from 0.659 to 0.414. Each figure is the mean of three seeds.
  • A single-number forecast fails as a warning system at 7 days (high-flow critical success index 0.005). Warnings taken from forecast quantiles reach 0.175. A model-free band around today's flow reaches 0.155, so the model's edge is narrow. At 7 days the single-number regional model has a high-flow CSI of 0.005, against 0.138 for persistence. Warning when a forecast quantile crosses the threshold gives 0.175, and a persistence band gives 0.155. The quantile was chosen on 2009 to 2012 only.
  • Most multi-day high-flow warnings are false alarms. For the quantile model 75 to 80% are wrong. For the two persistence baselines the share runs from 64% to 82%. High-flow false alarm ratio at 3 and 7 days: 0.753 and 0.802 for the quantile model, and between 0.635 and 0.816 for persistence and the persistence band.
  • The model's 90% range is honest where it was trained (coverage 0.90 to 0.91 after conformal calibration) and less so elsewhere: on unseen regions about one catchment in six gets a range that covers under 80%. After conformalized quantile regression on 2009 to 2012, the 90% interval covers 0.90 to 0.91 of days on seen catchments at every lead. On held-out regions the median catchment is at 0.89 to 0.90, but 14 to 18% of catchments fall under 0.80.

What went wrong, and what changed

  • The first diagnosis of a weak baseline (early stopping) was wrong. A controlled comparison on validation years showed the target scale was the cause. The log records both.
  • Twelve of 51 quantile models hit the tree limit, so those are under-trained. Reported, not hidden.

Limits

  • "Unseen" is not "ungauged": recent flow at the catchment is still an input.
  • The uncertainty experiment used one random seed. Coverage during floods specifically has not been checked.
  • Still to do: a neural sequence model, gaps in the inputs, and explanations.

Drug response beyond a single marker

Setup

  • 924 cancer cell lines. One gene, PTEN, is a known but incomplete guide to how they respond to drugs that block the AKT protein.
  • The question behind the design: how much more of the response does wider molecular data explain, and can the patterns be trusted?
  • 5-fold cross-validation repeated 5 times on saved folds. Every step that learns from data happens inside the training folds.
  • Cell lines are a stand-in. Nothing here is about patients or treatment.

Results

All numbers are held-out. R² is the share of the variation in response that a model explains.

  • Mostly a negative result. Wider data helps a little. More than four fifths of the response stays unexplained. Held-out R² rises from 0.112 (PTEN, cancer type and sequencing centre) to 0.144 with an elastic net on 2,000 genes and 0.187 with gradient boosting. The gains are +0.033 (95% interval +0.005 to +0.060) and +0.076 (+0.014 to +0.130). The difference between the two models, +0.043 (-0.003 to +0.086), is not clear of zero.
  • That gap is not mainly measurement noise. Where a related drug was measured twice, about 80% of the variation repeats, and the best model explains 18%. Exploratory. A related drug, Uprosertib, was measured twice at the same doses and the two measurements agree at 0.81 (0.76 to 0.85). The best model for that drug reaches R² 0.18. The ceiling is from the related drug, not from Ipatasertib.
  • A common mistake produces a believable result from noise. The false score is as large as the real one. Choosing genes by correlation with response on all cell lines, then cross-validating, gives R² 0.210 on the real response and 0.137 on a shuffled one. Choosing inside each fold gives -0.005 and -0.173.
  • The only stable subgroups are tissue of origin. Made-up data with no groups at all scores 0.80 to 0.87 on the usual stability measure when cut in two, so stability alone proves little. Among the 128 PTEN-altered cell lines, three clusters score stability 1.00 against 0.68 for reference data with no groups, and 94% of cell lines sit in the commonest cluster of their own tissue. After removing tissue and sequencing centre, no clustering is both stable and clearly above the reference.
  • Explanations change when the model is refitted. Only the original marker and one gene are reliably in the top 20. Top-20 feature lists from 25 refits (5 folds, 5 repeats) overlap by 0.20 on average (Jaccard index). Only PTEN status and one gene are in the top 20 at least 80% of the time.

What went wrong, and what changed

  • Gains were first computed on predictions averaged over repeats, which flattered the less stable model (+0.094 became +0.076). Corrected, with a test.
  • A sanity check declared in advance turned out nearly empty: 10 of the 12 pathway genes it named had been filtered out before the models saw them.
  • The pre-set bar for "stable clusters" had no reference to compare with. A no-groups reference was added before the run.
  • Three analyses were added after seeing results. They are labelled exploratory in the protocol and wherever they are reported.

Limits

  • One drug screen from one laboratory. No independent dataset.
  • Missing protein data was simulated at random. Models were not tuned.
  • No biological interpretation has been checked by a biologist.

Code, protocol and decision log: github.com/mcneillium/drug-response-beyond-marker

How the studies are run

  • Protocol firstThe design is written and dated before any model is fitted. Later changes are dated amendments.
  • Baselines firstEvery model is compared with the simplest thing that could work, on exactly the same held-out data.
  • Fixed splitsSaved once with a fingerprint. The code refuses to overwrite them.
  • Nothing learned from held-out dataFeature choice, scaling, gap filling and tuning all happen inside the training data.
  • Negative results reportedCriteria for "this did not work" are set in advance and reported against.
  • A log of every decisionIncluding the mistakes, how they were found and what was changed.
