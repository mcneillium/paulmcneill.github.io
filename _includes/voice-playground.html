This is Voice's core board and its word-prediction engine: the same code the app ships, running in this page with no server and no AI service. Tap words and watch the suggestions change. Each one says why it was offered.

To see it learn, switch on Learn from my messages, build a message such as I want + Grandma (type the name), press Speak two or three times, then clear and start again. Your words now appear among the suggestions, with a coloured border. Use × to stop a suggestion appearing after a word, and Forget to remove what it learned. Everything stays in this tab and is gone when you reload.

Switch on Natural voice to hear common phrases, such as I want to go home, in a recorded voice made with ElevenLabs. Other messages use your browser’s own voice.

The interactive board needs JavaScript. The full app demo below shows the same board.

How it works: a trigram model with absolute discounting, built from 666 sentences written for Voice (about 34 KB), merged with an optional personal layer whose counts halve every 45 days, then reordered by a small seven-feature ranker. Each set of suggestions takes well under a millisecond on a desktop computer. Read the prediction code.
